# Live close estimate and daily scoreboard (how to use)

Both tools are exploratory aids. They do not claim to predict direction or make money; the scoreboard exists to find out, on paper, over many sessions.

## Live close estimate (`src/live_nowcast.py`)
* `python3 src/live_nowcast.py` — fetches today's Yahoo 1-minute bars (unofficial, near-real-time), prints in plain words: price now, indicative close, 50% and 90% ranges, typical miss, and how the range will shrink if the price stays put. Logs the call to `results/nowcast_log.jsonl`.
* `--asof HH:MM` replays today as of that time; `--replay` prints the table used on the dashboard; `--backtest` runs the leave-one-session-out calibration; `--refresh` adds newly completed sessions to `data/processed/intraday_extra_15m.csv`.
* Method: centre = last traded price; spread = how much the stock usually moves from that time of day to the official (closing-auction) close, measured on ~39 sessions of 15-minute bars since the closing auction began. Today's volatility so far did not improve the leave-one-out test and is not used.
* Honest limits: small sample (39 sessions, overlapping checkpoints; 90% coverage could be 83-96%); the printed 50% range covers 50% overall but is too narrow in the morning; a late news spike breaks it (on 15 Oct the 12:30 and 13:15 calls ended outside the 90% range before the 13:30 jump); in this sample price rose about 0.2% from 15:00 to the close on average (not used). Independent review: research/live_verify_nowcast.md.

## Daily scoreboard (`src/scoreboard.py`)
* `issue` — run after the US close (~01:30 IST) and before 09:15 IST. Writes `results/scoreboard/entries/<date>.json` (never rewritten) with the open and close ranges, a direction lean (US S&P 500 last night + BSE's last two days), and two paper-trade rules: PT1 every day, PT2 only the strongest 20% of leans (open to close, 0.10% round-trip cost). **Commit the entry before 09:15 IST: the commit time is the proof.** Use `--dry` to preview.
* `score` — after the official NSE bhavcopy (~16:34 IST): outcomes, running totals, and comparison with "unchanged".
* `report` — running totals in plain words. 30 sessions cannot separate 55% from 50% (that takes several hundred): use it to catch gross failures and build evidence. `issue` refuses weekends, stale data (a missing weekday: pass `--force-gap` only for a known holiday) and anything after 09:00 IST on the target day (`--late` records a late entry). Independent review: research/live_verify_scoreboard.md.
* `src/official_bars.py` downloads missing official bars into `data/processed/bse_daily_extra.csv`. It assumes no corporate action: check `data/corp_actions_verified.csv` across any ex-date.

## Opening price from NSE's pre-open auction (`src/preopen.py`)
* `python3 src/preopen.py` reads NSE's pre-open call-auction data for BSE: the indicative opening price (IEP), orders waiting to buy and sell, and the book near the price. The IEP can still change until NSE closes order entry. Since 7 Sep 2026 (NSE circular NSE/CMTR/74969) entry runs 09:00-09:05 for limit and market orders and 09:05-09:10 for limit orders only, and it closes at a system-random moment in the last two minutes, i.e. anywhere from 09:08:00 to 09:10:00; matching and the opening price follow at once (to 09:12), buffer to 09:15. The price is FINAL only when NSE's `finalPrice` and `finalQuantity` fields are filled on today's page (the tool never decides by the clock); NSE uses it as the official open. `--wait-final 240` keeps reading until then; a page dated before today means holiday or not started (`STALE`, nothing saved). Snapshots are saved to `results/preopen/`.
* On 15 Oct the 09:09 pre-open price was Rs 3,342.0 and the official open was Rs 3,342.0 (miss Rs 0.0; one session, but the mechanism is that the auction sets the open). The endpoint is an unofficial public API and may change; it needs the cookie warm-up the script does.
* This gives the open to within a tick once it is FINAL (seen at 09:09:00 on 16 Oct and 09:09:34 on 15 Oct; the website feed lags the clock by about a minute). It is not a forecast, and it says nothing by itself about the direction of the rest of the day.

## Intraday study at 10:15 (`src/intraday_study.py`, exploratory)
Two years of hourly bars (486 sessions; 336 walk-forward observations). Nothing known at 10:15 (first-hour return, gap, Nifty and peers' first hour, BSE minus Nifty, all together) improved the estimate of the close beyond 'price stays': out-of-sample R2 -0.2% to -4.8%, typical miss about Rs 40 either way. Trade rules at 10:15 that looked good in the first 60% of the sample (follow the first-hour move: +0.8% per trade, t 3.3) were about zero in the last 40%. Hourly volume is unavailable (zero on most sessions).


## Hour-by-hour table (`src/hourly_table.py`): the user's fixed format
One row per time frame: time | precise close | close range at the end of the day | precise high of the next hour | precise low of the next hour | this hour's high vs the previous prediction | this hour's low vs the previous prediction. At 08:30 and 09:00 (before the open) `--pre-open` gives the open range and the open pin-point.
* `python3 src/hourly_table.py` — the row for now (Yahoo 1-minute bars, unofficial). Logs the predictions to `results/hourly_table_log.jsonl`. The 'this hour' columns compare ONLY the previous table given to the user (the latest live row logged before this one) with what happened in that table's own window ('so far only' while that hour is not over); with no earlier table the cell says so (no replay, no recomputed prediction). Separately every logged live prediction is scored once its own window is over (`--score`, `results/hourly_table_scored.csv`: the learning dataset; `--report` prints running accuracy). `--asof HH:MM` replays today for testing; `--eval` runs the leave-one-session-out test of the next-hour model (`results/hourly_hl_eval.csv`).
* Close pin-point = the price now (no drift); range = the live close estimate (`src/live_nowcast.py`).
* Next-hour high/low pin-point = the median of: P x exp(z x sigma_window), P = price now, sigma_window = the profile variance of the next 60 minutes (cut at 15:30), z = pooled standardised past excursions (about 39 sessions of 15-minute bars since the closing auction). Leave-one-session-out typical miss (rupees, mean absolute, 39 sessions; 31 for windows reaching 15:15): high Rs 6-15, low Rs 6-14 depending on the time of day, against Rs 10-22 for 'the high is the price now'. The 50% range held 36-65% and the 90% range 80-97% by checkpoint (39 sessions: noisy). Not validated beyond this; not a direction call; the new predictions have no live track record before 16 Oct 2026.
* "This hour" actual high and low are Yahoo 1-minute figures; NSE's official figures can differ by a few rupees.

## The day's high and low (`src/hl_model.py`, `src/live_highlow.py`): how precise, and what extra parameters did
* `python3 src/hl_model.py --date YYYY-MM-DD [--open PRICE]` — high and low of the day as a median (pin-point) with a "half the time" band and a "19 days in 20" band: before the open from the previous close, after the 09:08-09:09 open from the official open. Model: the project's volatility scale (S6) times filtered-historical-simulation quantiles of ln(H/P0), ln(L/P0) (and ln(H/O), ln(L/O) after the open). Walk-forward report: `research/hl/A_dayahead.md` and its independent check.
* `python3 src/live_highlow.py` — during the session: the running high/low so far and how far the final extremes usually go beyond them. Its "another Rs X-Y typical" line and "chance of at least Rs X" are not reliable (checker finding, `research/hl/verify_B_liveintraday.md`); it ignores the official open, so the floor can be wrong on gap-open days.
* **Precision (out of sample, 1 Jul 2025 to 15 Oct 2026, n = 320, rupees at Rs 3,428):** the typical miss of the median is Rs 42.7 (high) / 47.7 (low) before the open and Rs 34.8 / 37.4 once the official open is known; "price stays" would miss by Rs 62.9 / 61.3. About one day in twenty a band is missed on each side by design.
* **More parameters did not sharpen it (three independent studies, each checked by a separate reviewer, 18 Oct 2026):** D (VIX, US overnight, Indian market and peers, BSE own history, calendar, implied vol; 46 variant-target pairs), E (quantile regression, boosting, forest, conformal, stacks; 72 pairs), G (analogue days "watch the past"; 172 pairs). **0 of 290 pairs met the pre-fixed rule** (at least 2% lower pinball with Holm p < 0.05, same sign in Validation, coverage not worse, no loss in the closing-auction slice, leakage test); the largest rupee gains were about Rs 1 on the typical miss, inside the standard error (about Rs 2-3). This means "no gain demonstrated", not "no gain exists": with 320 sessions only gains of roughly 3% or more could have been confirmed. The baseline stays the headline. Reports: `research/hl/D_features.md`, `E_quantile.md`, `G_analogue.md`, and `verify_*.md`.
* **One lead, not a finding:** the S&P 500's last session as a shift of the whole pre-open band (post hoc in D: +2.1% high / +2.9% low pinball, Holm 1.00 / 0.25 over 92 pairs; E's correctly timed US overnight variant: +2.86% / +1.67%, Holm 1.0). Same direction in both; neither is significant. Not used in any forecast.
* **Opening price final?** `src/preopen.py` now calls the NSE pre-open price FINAL only when NSE's `finalPrice` field is filled (15 Oct: stamped 09:09:34; 16 Oct: stamped 09:09:00; a 09:08:34 snapshot of 16 Oct had the right IEP but finalPrice 0). Secondary sources say the pre-open timetable changed on 7 Sep 2026; unconfirmed (see `research/hl/F_data_availability.md`).

## Every reading in a CSV (`src/readings_csv.py`, `results/readings/`)
* `results/readings/readings.csv` — all days, one row per reading (08:30 open range, NSE pre-open snapshots, day-ahead and after-open high/low, every hour-by-hour table with the row as printed); `results/readings/by_day/<date>.csv` — the same rows for one day; column dictionary in `results/readings/README.md`.
* Rebuilt from the logs (`hourly_table_log.jsonl`, `preopen/`, `scoreboard/entries/`, `hl_dayahead_*.json`, `hourly_table_scored.csv`, the official bars), after every table, pre-open reading, saved high/low forecast and at the 16:45 scoring, so the file cannot differ from what was shown. Scoring columns (actual high/low of the window, errors, inside the half-the-time / 9-in-10 ranges, official open / high / low / close) are filled in as the facts arrive.

## Peers' move column and forward test H (`src/peers_live.py`, `src/peer_forward.py`, `research/hl/H_peer_forward.md`)
* **From Thu 22 Oct 2026 a live table row has an eighth column, "peers' move today vs BSE's":** the average move since the previous close of CDSL, MCX, MOTILALOFS and IEX (each shown), BSE's own move, the gap (peers minus BSE, in points) and Nifty's move. **Context only: no forecast, pin-point or range uses it.** Moves are 100 x ln(price / previous close) from Yahoo 1-minute bars that started before the reading's minute (unofficial; previous close = Yahoo `chartPreviousClose`); a peer with no bar, a bar older than 10 minutes, or a move above 15% is dropped; fewer than 3 of 4 peers gives "n/a". Rows logged before 22 Oct and replays (`--asof`) print the original seven columns exactly as before. `python3 src/peers_live.py [--asof HH:MM]` shows the snapshot (inspection only, never logged).
* **The test (pre-registered before any forward data, sha256 in `docs/DEVIATIONS.md` #42):** does the gap say anything about how far BSE still moves from a reading to the official close? One primary reading per session (the first at or after 11:00 and before 13:00), one-sided permutation test on the slope, Holm over two baskets, adoption only with an out-of-sample absolute error at least 3% below "price stays" and the same sign in both halves. **Results are LOCKED until 60 eligible sessions (about three months); one more look at 120.** Until then `hourly_table.py --score` (the 16:45 job) rebuilds `results/peer_forward/dataset.csv` and prints one "Forward test H" line with counts and data problems only.
* **What the test can and cannot show:** simulated power at 60 sessions is 15% to 98% depending on the (unmeasured) size of the effect, and the full three-condition rule fires about 77% of the time when the true slope is 0.6 with a gap sd of 1.0 point (about 1% under the null, 300 synthetic runs). "Not demonstrated" is the likely outcome and would not show that no relationship exists. `python3 src/peer_forward.py --selftest` runs the analysis on synthetic data only (null, built-in effect, reverse sign, no look-ahead, stage gate).
* **21 Oct is excluded.** It motivated the idea (BSE -2.63%, every close reading above the official close, the capital-market peers down about as much). Replaying the column at that day's readings (inspection only, after the definitions were frozen): the gap was -0.13, +0.07, -0.97, -1.32, -0.56, -0.84 and -0.28 points at 09:41, 10:12, 11:16, 12:04, 13:12, 14:12 and 15:13, i.e. the peers had fallen further than BSE for most of the day, which fits catch-up on that one day but is one day chosen after the fact and proves nothing.
* The readings CSV gains `bse_move_pct`, `peers_move_pct`, `peers_minus_bse_pp`, `nifty_move_pct`, `peers_valid` and `remaining_move_to_close_pct` (100 x ln(official close / price now), the outcome of the test, filled for every table row once the official bar is in).
* **On the dashboard** (hour-by-hour table section): a "Peers vs BSE since previous close" column, filled from `dashboard/data/hourly_rows.json` (`peers_move`, `bse_move`, `peers_gap`, `nifty_move`; built by `src/build_dashboard_data.py`, re-uploaded by the 16:45 job). Rows logged before 22 Oct show a dash. The section note no longer carries the 16 Oct wording. `dashboard/page.html` is now the copy of the page as published. The dashboard shows the per-reading peer figures only; it does not show the remaining move to the close next to them, so the locked test is not previewed there.
* **26 Oct correction (DEVIATIONS 43):** the opening price printed by `preopen.py`, `hourly_table.py --pre-open` and stored in the readings CSV is now the equilibrium price (IEP) of NSE's page, never its `finalPrice` field (on 26 Oct that field held the previous close, Rs 3,230.8, while the equilibrium price and the official open were Rs 3,213.0). "FINAL" still means the auction has matched (finalPrice and finalQuantity filled). A warning line is printed when the two disagree. The dashboard's after-open row is recomputed from the official open once NSE's bar is in, so for 26 Oct it shows the corrected forecast (open Rs 3,213.0), not the one given at 09:09 (built on Rs 3,230.8, flagged in the readings CSV).
